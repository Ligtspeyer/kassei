⚙️ The Core Mechanism

FRO's gain isn't simply "the strait is closed." It operates through two reinforcing channels:

1. Route lengthening: Ships that can't or won't transit Hormuz take the Cape of Good Hope route (adding ~10–14 days per round trip). This consumes vessel-days without adding vessels, tightening the global tanker market — including routes nowhere near Iran. This is why VLCC rates globally surged, not just Gulf-specific ones.

2. Insurance-driven capacity destruction: When P&I insurers pull war-risk coverage, tankers physically capable of transiting simply won't. This is more powerful than physical obstruction because it acts immediately and globally — any tanker whose owner can't secure war-risk cover is effectively removed from the Hormuz route.

Key nuance: FRO's own fleet reportedly hasn't been transiting the strait at all. Every dollar of earnings upside came from the market-wide rate environment, not from FRO assuming direct war risk. That's the clean asymmetric trade: exposure to the rate spike without exposure to the kinetic risk.
